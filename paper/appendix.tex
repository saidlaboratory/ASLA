\section{Proofs}
\label{app:design}

\paragraph{Setting.} Candidates $i$ are fitted on one shared design: budgets
$C_1,\dots,C_k$ with $n_j$ runs each. Write $x_j=(1,-\log C_j)^\top$ and  %% nolint: mathematical constant
$A(n)=\sum_j n_j x_j x_j^\top$, assumed nonsingular. Observations are
$y_{ijr}=x_j^\top\theta_i+\varepsilon_{ijr}$ in log space, with $\theta_i$
estimated by least squares.

\paragraph{Assumptions.} (A1) Every candidate uses the same design $n$.
(A2) The errors have mean zero and variance $\sigma^2$, and are independent  %% nolint: mathematical constant
across runs and across candidates. (A3) The model is well specified: there is
no deviation $b(C)$ from the linear form.

\begin{theorem}
Under (A1)--(A3), for every pair $(i,i')$ and every design $n$,
$\operatorname{Var}\bigl(x_*^\top(\hat\theta_i-\hat\theta_{i'})\bigr)
 = 2\operatorname{Var}\bigl(x_*^\top\hat\theta_i\bigr) = 2\sigma^2 v(n)$ with  %% nolint: mathematical constant
$v(n)=x_*^\top A(n)^{-1}x_*$. Over any feasible set of designs the variance of  %% nolint: mathematical constant
the target gap and the variance of the target level have the same minimisers,
for all pairs at once. If the errors are Gaussian, the probability that the
estimated gap has the wrong sign is
$\Phi\bigl(-|\Delta_{ii'}|/\sqrt{2\sigma^2 v(n)}\bigr)$, increasing in $v(n)$,  %% nolint: mathematical constant
so the level-optimal design minimises every pair's mis-selection probability.
\end{theorem}

\paragraph{Proof.} Least squares gives
$\hat\theta_i-\theta_i=A(n)^{-1}\sum_j x_j\sum_r\varepsilon_{ijr}$, so by (A2)  %% nolint: mathematical constant
$\operatorname{Cov}(\hat\theta_i)=\sigma^2A(n)^{-1}$. Independence across  %% nolint: mathematical constant
candidates gives $\operatorname{Cov}(\hat\theta_i-\hat\theta_{i'})=2\sigma^2A(n)^{-1}$.  %% nolint: mathematical constant
The pair variance is twice the level variance, a factor that does not depend on
$n$, so the minimisers coincide. By (A3) the estimated gap is unbiased; under
Gaussian errors it is $\mathcal N(\Delta_{ii'},2\sigma^2v(n))$, and since $\Phi$  %% nolint: mathematical constant
is increasing and $|\Delta_{ii'}|$ does not depend on $n$, the wrong-sign
probability is increasing in $v(n)$. \hfill$\square$

\paragraph{What the assumptions exclude.} Budget-dependent variance shared by
all candidates is harmless. The conclusion fails when candidates use different
designs, when the correlation between candidates' errors varies with budget,
and when the model is misspecified: a deviation $b(C)$ adds a bias that depends
on $n$. The measurements in \cref{sec:lever} show the last case holds in
practice. The theorem is about design; it does not say whether projection
should beat single-scale ranking. A region objective that averages prediction
variance around the target correlates with the point objective at \thmCorr\ over
\thmDesigns\ random designs; the design optimal for a region spanning
\thmRegionDecades\ decades costs \thmExcessThree\% extra point variance.

\section{Correcting a pair-resampled interval}
\label{app:correction}

The pair-resampled variance of a pair mean over $n$ candidates is
$\zeta_2/\binom{n}{2}$, and the candidate-level variance is  %% nolint: binomial coefficient notation
$2\bigl(2(n-2)\zeta_1+\zeta_2\bigr)/\bigl(n(n-1)\bigr)$. Their standard-error  %% nolint: mathematical constant
ratio is $R(n)=\sqrt{1+2(n-2)\zeta_1/\zeta_2}$. On the C4 comparison  %% nolint: mathematical constant
$R(\nCandidates)=\curveCFour$ from the formula, against the ratio
\dependenceFactor\ of the two standard errors computed directly
(\cref{sec:test}). Across \curveComparisons\ comparisons in this
paper and the published re-tests the median $\zeta_1/\zeta_2$ is
\curveZetaMedian, giving $R=\curveMedTen$, \curveMedTwentyFive\ and
\curveMedHundred\ at \curveMSmall, \curveMMid\ and \curveMLarge\ candidates.
Subsampling the real candidates reproduces $R(m)$ to within
\curveCheckWorst\% at every $m$ from \curveCheckMinM\ to \curveCheckMaxM. A
first version of this check divided by the mean of the subsamples'
pair-resampled standard errors instead of their root mean square; the
heavy-tailed C4 kernel pulls the mean well below the root mean square at small
$m$, and that version reported a \curveCheckMeanSeSmallest\% miss at
$m=\curveCheckSmallestM$ and a failed pre-registered prediction
(\cref{app:practice}). The mean-based ratio still describes a typical small
study's pair-resampled interval, which understates more than $R(m)$ suggests.

\section{Mechanism experiments}
\label{app:mechanism}



Arm A's noiseless truth is an exact power law for every recipe, so projection
captures every crossover and its excess falls with the lever arm. Noise alone
reverses the sign. Arm B2's recipe-specific misspecification reverses it without
noise. Arm B1's common-mode misspecification changes the noisy result little but moves
the noiseless correlation (\cref{tab:arms}). With
the noise removed from the real shrunk curves, projection is wrong on more pairs
than single-scale ranking at every one of \mechNoiselessDesigns\ designs.

\section{Certifying selection: full results}
\label{app:stage}

\begin{algorithm}[h]
\caption{CERTIFY, as pre-registered.}
\label{alg:certify}
\begin{algorithmic}
\STATE \textbf{input:} candidates $i$; fit rungs with compute $C_r$, values $y_{ir}$ and standard errors $s_{ir}$; target $C_T$; forms $F$; level $\alpha$; misfit coefficients $\kappa_f$
\FOR{each form $f\in F$ and candidate $i$}
\STATE $\hat y_{if}\gets$ the form-$f$ forecast at $C_T$; $g_{if}\gets\partial\hat y_{if}/\partial y_{ir}$ by finite differences
\STATE $L_i\gets\log_{10}(C_T/\max_r C_r)$  %% nolint: the base of the logarithm
\STATE $v_{if}\gets\sum_r g_{ifr}^2 s_{ir}^2+(\kappa_f L_i\hat y_{if})^2$  %% nolint: squares in a variance formula
\ENDFOR
\FOR{each pair $(i,j)$}
\STATE certify ``$i$ before $j$'' iff for every $f$: $\hat y_{if}<\hat y_{jf}$ and $\Phi\bigl((\hat y_{jf}-\hat y_{if})/\sqrt{v_{if}+v_{jf}}\bigr)\ge 1-\alpha$  %% nolint: algebra
\ENDFOR
\STATE \textbf{output:} the certified partial order and its maximal elements
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\paragraph{Fixed choices.} $\alpha=\stageAlpha\%$. The form set is log-log and an
offset power law fitted by least squares from fixed starts. $\kappa_f$ is
estimated by moment matching on held-out rungs strictly between a ladder's top
and the evaluation target, with the common mode removed, leave-one-ladder-out.
Seed error on DataDecide is the moderated standard error of the seed mean.
Baselines: single-scale ranking and projection, which always decide; the same
rule with $\kappa_f=0$; a seed bootstrap;  %% nolint: the misfit term switched off and split conformal intervals with
scores normalised by the lever arm. Charges use the reference-uncertainty
treatment of \cref{sec:setup}.



\paragraph{The criterion.} Realized false certification at or below nominal
(held, \stageSelFcr\%) and higher coverage than every baseline that also meets
nominal (failed: seed error alone met nominal pooled at \stageSeedFcr\% with
\stageSeedCov\% coverage). The possibly-best set averaged \stageSelSetSize\
recipes and contained the true best with probability \stageSelBest, against
\stageSeedBest\ for seed error alone and \stageSingleBest\ for single-scale
ranking.

\paragraph{Deviations.} There are three, all disclosed in the results file. On Marin the  %% words: - (a count of the deviations listed below)
offset power law is not identified on the two calibration rungs, so its $\kappa$
was transferred from DataDecide C4; the log-log $\kappa$ estimated there was
\stageMarinKappaLog. Conformal intervals on Marin were calibrated on log-log
residuals for the same reason. A first run drew all designs from one random
stream, so results depended on which designs were cached; each design now has
its own seeded generator, and cold and cached runs are byte-identical.

\paragraph{Linearization check.} The Gaussian-limit decisions agree with
full-refit draws on \stageLinMin--\stageLinMax\% of ordered pairs on the checked
designs.

\section{The Marin leaderboard: details}
\label{app:marin}

\paragraph{The noise bracket.} The low end comes from borrowed seed standard
deviations: DataDecide at \targetScaleLabel\ (\noiseLowDataDecide\%) and Delphi's
held-out runs (\noiseLowDelphiA\% and \noiseLowDelphiB\%). A cross-hardware pair
of identical configurations differs by \noiseHardware\% (\noiseHardwareDf\ df),
but shares its seed, so it measures hardware divergence, not seed noise. The
high end comes from leave-one-out residuals of public hyperparameter sweeps and
isoFLOP curves: \marinBoundLow--\marinBoundHigh\% by size, and
\noiseHighDelphiMin--\noiseHighDelphiMax\% from the isoFLOP curves alone. The
residual of one submission's learning-rate sweep is \noiseSweepResidual\%
(\noiseSweepDf\ df). Every value includes lack of fit and is an upper bound.

\paragraph{Solver.} Raw FLOPs make the fit's Jacobian columns nearly collinear,
and an absolute error tolerance discards the final improvements when the sum of
squares is small. On the earlier snapshot, without rescaling,
\marinUnscaledReached\ multi-start fits reach the optimum even at a tight
tolerance.

\paragraph{The class comparison, inconclusive.} Restricted to pairs of
genuinely different recipes trained at identical compute with identical
per-size protocol changes, \marinMatchedReordered\ of \marinMatchedDetermined\
determined pairs reorder across rung steps. Detecting a \marinPowerDelta-point
difference from DataDecide's rate at power 0.8 needs \marinPowerN\ determined  %% nolint: stated power level
pairs (\marinPowerNHigh\ against a higher reference rate); one Marin design
provides at most \marinMaxDetermined. Fitted optima from the Fantastic Optimizers
sweep \citep{wen2025fantastic}, on the same size ladder, reorder
\marinFoReordered\ of \marinFoDetermined\ determined pairs across the rung step
where most submissions change protocol.

\section{Instability across metrics and the regime map}
\label{app:regime}

A regime map relates noise, misspecification and candidate spread to
projection's excess. At C4's coordinates it gives \mapExcessCFourPrimary\
points on the primary design, close to the real \excessObserved. At OLMES's
coordinates it gives \mapExcessOlmesPrimary, while worlds built at those
coordinates give \worldExcessOlmesPrimary. Moving one metric's worlds to
another's dimensionless coordinates predicted the sign of the other's excess in
\transferMatchesBase\ of \transferTestsBase\ tests. We report the map as
descriptive of DataDecide; it does not transfer between metrics. Under a flatter noise profile than DataDecide's, the
fixed-candidate procedure covers \calFlatFixedCov\ against
\calControlFixedCov\ in the control.

\section{Reference uncertainty}
\label{app:reference}

\paragraph{No detected seed dependence across scales.} Across \couplingPairs\ pairs
of scales the correlation between a seed's deviations has median
\couplingMedianCFour\ on C4 and \couplingMedianErr\ on OLMES error; permutation
tests reject at \couplingRejectCFour\% and \couplingRejectErr\% of pairs. With
\ddSeeds\ seeds per recipe these tests have little power, so this is an
absence of detected dependence, not a demonstration of independence.

\paragraph{Moderation.} On PolyPythias \citep{vanderwal2025polypythias}, with
tasks in the role of recipes, moderation reduces the error in log standard
deviation at \polyBetter\ of \polyCells\ cells (median \polyRmseRaw\ to
\polyRmseMod) and only partly removes the low bias of a small-sample standard
deviation (\polyRatioRaw\ to \polyRatioMod\ of the truth). The adopted weight
scored the lowest Brier score of \ppWeights\ candidates (\brierAdopted, against
\brierModT\ with prior degrees of freedom and \brierRaw\ with raw standard
errors); this choice was not pre-registered.

\paragraph{A published figure.} DataDecide's pairwise decision accuracy for
ranking at one small scale is \knownAnswerPublished; reproduced per seed, as
published, it is \knownAnswerPerSeed, and against the seed mean, which the
corrections below use, it is \publishedOverall. Only \pubDetFrac\% of its reference pairs are determined
under Bonferroni. Posterior expected accuracy gives \pubPosterior;
disattenuated accuracy, which assumes the rule's errors are independent of the
reference's, gives \pubDisattenuated. Both err where true gaps are small
(reference reliability \relSmallGaps\ on the smallest-gap third against
\relLargeGaps\ on the largest), so the correction's direction is not identified.

\paragraph{Resolvability.} With per-pair Welch degrees of freedom, a normal
quantile under Bonferroni is anti-conservative by a median factor of
\tRatioMedian\ (10th to 90th percentile across pairs:  %% nolint: percentile names
\tRatioPTen--\tRatioPNinety).

\section{The practice record}
\label{app:practice}

Several results in this project were at first wrong because a measurement could
not see the quantity it claimed to measure. The full record, with each case's
detection path, is generated in \texttt{results/consolidated/METHODS\_PRACTICE.md}.
The cases reported in this paper are: an earlier headline count that did not
regenerate from committed code (\shippedProjFlips\ projection errors shipped,
\recomputedProjFlips\ reproducible); a residual-scatter ratio first reported as
\residRatioSuperseded\ and corrected to \residRatio\ after a pooling error; a
perturbation that moved the simulated truth but not the simulated data;
comparisons at a named model size that were not at equal compute; and an
official leaderboard submission silently dropped by label parsing. The detection
rules that caught them are reconstructing every number from committed inputs,
checking that a perturbation built to move an estimate moves it, and counting a
population against its source.
