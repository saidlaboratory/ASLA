\begin{abstract}
Small training runs are used to choose between training recipes, either by
ranking the recipes at the largest small scale or by fitting a scaling law to
each and extrapolating. We ask whether extrapolation chooses better, and we
measure the answer as decision accuracy against an ordering at a larger target.
Answering it needs a test whose unit is the candidate recipe rather than the
pair, with critical values calibrated by simulation, because standard tests
are anti-conservative at realistic seed and candidate counts. On DataDecide,
projection's excess mis-selection over single-scale ranking on C4 is
\headEstimate\ points: nominally significant for these \nCandidates\ recipes
($p=\headFixedP$, not surviving correction within its family) and not
established for recipes in general ($p=\headRandomP$). Of the
\restatedPrimaryM\ claims in the paper's primary family, one survives Holm and
Benjamini--Yekutieli correction: on C4, projection's excess rises with
extrapolation distance (Spearman \leverRealRho). A pre-registered confound
test over \confDesigns\ fit windows, reported outside that family, adjusts for
the size of the largest fitted model and the number of fitted sizes: in a linear
model the excess rises by \confJointLever\ points per decade of distance
(interval [\confJointLeverWaldLow, \confJointLeverWaldHigh]), and a smaller
largest model and fewer fitted sizes are each associated with a larger excess.
With those two fixed, distance varies only through the target, so the test
cannot separate distance from target size, and the rise is not linear. No
tested alternative is shown to be better than single-scale ranking; the
intervals exclude an advantage larger than \advExcludeCFourMax\ points on C4,
and the estimator tests have power below one half. On C4, certification from
seed error alone has a false-certification rate of \stageCFourSeedFcr\%; pooled,
it exceeds nominal on OLMES error (\stageErrSeedFcr\%). On the Marin speedrun leaderboard, a solver defect and per-size
protocol changes limit what the forecasts resolve, and at the upper end of a
bracket on single-run noise \marinHold\ of \marinPairs\ pairwise orders hold.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

A team choosing between data mixtures, architectures or optimizers cannot
train every candidate at full scale. It trains each at a few small budgets and
picks the candidate that looks best. Two rules are common. \emph{Single-scale
ranking} orders candidates by their score at the largest small budget.
\emph{Projection} fits a scaling law to each candidate's small runs and orders
them by the extrapolated score at the target budget
\citep{kaplan2020scaling,hoffmann2022training}. Projection should help when
candidates cross between the small budgets and the target, and it should hurt
when the fitted curves are wrong.

Which effect dominates is a question about decisions, not about fit
accuracy. A scaling law can fit every candidate to within a percent and still
order two close candidates wrongly, and a poorly fitting law can order them
correctly. We therefore measure selection rules by decision accuracy: the
fraction of candidate pairs whose predicted order matches an order measured at
the target \citep{magnusson2025datadecide,heineman2025signal}.

We make four contributions.  %% words: - (the outline of this paper)
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{A decision-level test.} Decision accuracy is a mean over pairs that
share candidates, so its unit of inference is the candidate. We give a
candidate-level U-statistic test with critical values calibrated on a
semi-synthetic benchmark, show how anti-conservative the standard alternatives
are, and use it to re-examine DataDecide's comparisons (\cref{sec:test}).
\item \emph{The lever-arm effect.} On C4, projection's excess error over
single-scale ranking rises with the distance it extrapolates. This is the only
claim in the paper that survives family-wide correction. A pre-registered
confound test finds it after linear adjustment for the size of the largest
fitted model and the number of fitted sizes, which are also associated with the
excess; it cannot separate distance from the size of the target.
Semi-synthetic experiments and measurements of the error structure are
consistent with noise and recipe-specific misspecification as its sources; on
OLMES accuracy metrics it is not established (\cref{sec:lever}).
\item \emph{Selection and uncertainty.} No tested alternative is shown to be
better than single-scale ranking; we report the advantages the data exclude and
the power of each test. We describe false certification by metric and
extrapolation distance (\cref{sec:consequences}).
\item \emph{A case study.} The Marin speedrun leaderboard forecasts each
submission's loss at a far target. A solver defect, single-run noise and
protocol changes between rungs determine what it can resolve
(\cref{sec:marin}).
\end{enumerate}

In summary, no rule we tested was shown to be better than single-scale
ranking on the data we could test, and on C4 projection's deficit grows with how
far the target is. The gap between the two rules is nominally significant for
the recipes measured and too small to establish for recipes in general with the
candidate counts public suites provide.

\section{Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{Ladders, candidates and targets.} A \emph{ladder} is a set of fit
budgets; a \emph{design} is a ladder and a target budget above it. The
\emph{lever arm} of a design is $\log_{10}$ of the ratio of target compute to  %% nolint: the base of the logarithm
the largest fitted compute. Our main data are DataDecide's
\citep{magnusson2025datadecide} \nCandidates\ pretraining-data recipes, each
trained at fourteen sizes with \ddSeeds\ seeds, evaluated on C4 bits per token  %% words: - (the number of model sizes in DataDecide's release)
and on OLMES macro error and correct-answer probability. The Marin speedrun
\citep{marin2025speedrun} supplies a second, deployed setting.

\paragraph{Scoring against an uncertain reference.} The target order is itself
estimated from finitely many seeds. We charge each pair the probability that
its true order differs from the predicted one, under a Gaussian approximation
to the target gap with seed variances moderated across recipes by empirical
Bayes \citep{smyth2004linear}. A pair whose reference is a coin flip then costs
about one half whatever the rule predicts, instead of rewarding or punishing
it by chance.

\paragraph{Two estimands.} A difference in decision accuracy between two rules
answers one of two questions. The \emph{fixed-candidate} estimand asks whether
the difference is real for these recipes; only seed noise is random, and we
resample it parametrically. The \emph{random-candidate} estimand asks whether
it would hold for new recipes; the recipes are a sample, and the uncertainty is
candidate-level. Reported intervals state which estimand they cover.

\section{A decision-level test}
\label{sec:test}

\paragraph{The statistic and its unit.} Over $n$ candidates, decision accuracy
averages a kernel $h(i,j)$ over the $\binom{n}{2}$ pairs, so it is a  %% nolint: binomial coefficient notation
U-statistic of order two \citep{hoeffding1948class}. Its variance has a
candidate-level component $\zeta_1$ and a pair-level component $\zeta_2$.
Treating pairs as independent keeps only the second. On the C4 comparison the
pair-only interval understates the standard error by a factor of
\dependenceFactor\ and would have reported a significant difference
($p=\expectedPairP$) where the candidate-level test does not
($p=\expectedCandP$). The ratio of the two standard errors is
$R(n)=\sqrt{1+2(n-2)\zeta_1/\zeta_2}$, which grows with $n$: more candidates  %% nolint: closed-form expression
make pair resampling more anti-conservative, not less
(\cref{app:correction}).

\paragraph{Calibration.} We built a semi-synthetic benchmark from DataDecide:
truths are the recipes' seed-mean curves shrunk toward the across-recipe mean,
noise follows the moderated model, and random-candidate worlds draw new recipe
sets by a smoothed bootstrap. In \calRandomWorlds\ random-candidate and
\calFixedWorlds\ fixed-candidate worlds, unbiased candidate-level standard
errors gave Wald intervals covering only \calUCovMin--\calUCovMax, because the
standard errors are noisy at \nCandidates\ candidates (\cref{tab:calibration}).
Calibrated critical
values improve coverage: \calFixedK\ for the fixed-candidate estimand and
\calJackK\ for the random-candidate one, with held-out coverage
\calFixedCovMin--\calFixedCovMax\ and \calJackCovMin--\calJackCovMax, short of
the pre-registered band for both (\cref{app:ledger}). Two
standard tools fail on real few-seed data. A moderated $t$ test splitting
PolyPythias' \ppSeeds\ seeds \ppNullSplit\ against \ppNullSplit, where the null
is true, rejects at \nullModBonf\ times its nominal rate at a Bonferroni level
\citep{vanderwal2025polypythias}. With per-pair Welch degrees of freedom, the
normal quantile under Bonferroni correction is anti-conservative by a median
factor of \tRatioMedian\ across pairs. Calibration on the benchmark was not pre-registered;
it was adopted after intervals at the textbook value undercovered, and both
versions are reported.

\paragraph{The headline gap at the candidate level.} On DataDecide's primary
C4 design (fit rungs up to \primaryFitTop, target \primaryTarget), projection mis-selects
\headEstimate\ points more pairs than single-scale ranking. For these
\nCandidates\ recipes the difference is nominally significant: the
fixed-candidate interval is [\headFixedLow, \headFixedHigh] ($p=\headFixedP$),
but it \headFixedFamilyVerdict\ Holm or BY correction within the fixed-candidate
family (\cref{tab:claims-fixed}), and this estimand covered at
\calProjFixedCov\ for projection in the benchmark. For recipes in general it is
not established: [\headRandomLow, \headRandomHigh] ($p=\headRandomP$), and the
calibrated random-candidate test has power \calHeadRandomPower\ for this
comparison (\cref{tab:calibration}).
Detecting a two-point difference at power 0.8 under the calibrated test needs  %% nolint: stated power level
about \candShrunkTwo\ candidates, several times what public suites provide.

\paragraph{The paper's claims under correction.} We restate every inferential
claim in the paper, \restatedPrimaryM\ in all, under the random-candidate
procedure and correct the family with Holm \citep{holm1979simple} and
Benjamini--Yekutieli \citep{benjamini2001control}.
\restatedPrimaryNominal\ are nominally significant and \restatedPrimaryBY\
survives: the lever-arm association of \cref{sec:lever}. The full table is in
\cref{app:claims}.

\paragraph{DataDecide, re-examined.} DataDecide's own decision-accuracy
function, run unmodified on its released predictions, reproduces
\codeCheckTested\ of \codeCheckTestedN\ tested rows and \codeCheckAll\ of
\codeCheckAllN\ overall. The remaining \ddRemainingRows\ depend on how its sort
orders tied predictions: \tieGroups\ groups of tied values admit orderings that
move a row by up to \tieMaxRange\ points. The protocol mixes targets: it scores
single-scale ranking against the \ddSeeds-seed mean at \targetScaleLabel\ and
each scaling-law variant against the default seed. Under the mixed targets,
\ddFindingVariantName\ scores \ddFindingVariantDefault\ against single-scale
ranking's \ddFindingSingle. Scored against one consistent target, the seed
mean, it reaches \ddFindingVariant: the point estimates reverse. The difference,
\ddFindingDiff\ points, lies inside its uncalibrated candidate-level interval
([\ddFindingLow, \ddFindingHigh]), so neither order is established. Against the
other consistent target, the default seed for both rules, the variant stays
behind (\ddFindingDefaultBoth\ points), so the direction depends on the
target. The
published conclusion that no variant exceeds single-scale ranking is consistent
with the data; equivalence is not established.

\section{When extrapolation fails: the lever-arm effect}
\label{sec:lever}

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{figures/fig_lever_arm}
\caption{Projection's excess mis-selection over single-scale ranking on C4,
under expected-error scoring, against the lever arm of each of

\label{fig:lever}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{The regularity.} On C4, across \doseDesigns\ designs, projection's
excess over single-scale ranking rises with the lever arm (\cref{fig:lever};
Spearman \leverRealRho). The designs share recipes and rungs, so we test it
with recipes as the unit: a leave-one-recipe-out jackknife of Fisher's $z$ gives
[\leverWaldLow, \leverWaldHigh] at the normal critical value and
[\leverRealLow, \leverRealHigh] at a critical value of \leverK\ calibrated on
benchmark worlds whose true correlation (\leverRhoTrue) is below the estimate,
so neither interval certifies the size of the effect. In those worlds the
estimator is biased low (mean \leverCalMeanRhoHat\ against \leverRhoTrue), the
normal interval covered at \leverCalCovWald, and the pre-registered coverage
band \leverCalCovPred. A null in which a comparator's true excess is zero at
every design, while designs that share a rung share its noise, rejects in
\leverNullRate\ of \leverNullN\ independent draws at the test's level; none
reaches the real statistic ($p\le\leverRealP$, one over the number of draws). Because a rank correlation is unchanged by a constant
shift, this null also stands for an excess that is positive but the same at
every design. It does not stand for an excess that varies with the size of the
top rung or the number of fitted rungs: every one of these designs fits from the
smallest model up, so long lever arms come only from small top rungs and the rung
count is fixed by the top rung. Without noise, on the shrinkage-estimated
curves, projection is wrong on \mechNoiselessProjWrong\ pairs against
single-scale ranking's \mechNoiselessSingleWrong\ summed over the overlapping
designs, and beats it at \mechNoiselessProjBeats\ of \mechNoiselessDesigns.

\paragraph{Separating lever arm from ladder size.} A pre-registered confound
test breaks that link with fit windows: any run of at least three consecutive  %% words: - (the pre-registered minimum window)
rungs, and any larger target, which gives \confDesigns\ designs scored the same
way. Three factorial slices each vary one factor with the other two fixed:  %% words: - (the three factors)
in slice (i), \confSliceLeverN\ slices vary the target with the window fixed
(lever arm only); in slice (ii), \confSliceTopN\ move top rung and target
together at a fixed rung count and a lever arm matched within a fifth of a decade
(top-rung size only, with the residual lever arm as a covariate); in slice
(iii), \confSliceRungsN\ vary the window's lower end with top rung and target
fixed (rung count only). A joint model regresses the excess linearly on lever
arm, top-rung compute and rung count over all designs. Standard errors are
leave-one-recipe-out jackknifes; critical values (\confKMin--\confKMax) are
calibrated in \confWorlds\ random-candidate worlds built as for the lever-arm
test, and \confKBelow\ of \confKTotal\ are below the normal value, so here
calibration narrows the intervals; we quote the wider normal intervals. The
pre-registered outcome is that the \confOutcome, and the other two factors also
contribute (\cref{tab:confound}). In the joint model the excess rises by
\confJointLever\ points per decade of lever arm
([\confJointLeverWaldLow, \confJointLeverWaldHigh]), and in slice (i) by
\confSliceLever\ ([\confSliceLeverWaldLow, \confSliceLeverWaldHigh]). It falls
with top-rung compute (\confJointTop\ points per decade,
[\confJointTopWaldLow, \confJointTopWaldHigh]) and with rung count
(\confJointRungs\ per rung, [\confJointRungsWaldLow, \confJointRungsWaldHigh]);
the slices agree in sign. The predictions that rung count would not matter
failed (\confPredFailed); \confPredHeld\ of \confPredTotal\ held. These are
associations within one ladder, outside the corrected family, and they have
three limits. First, with the top rung fixed, lever arm changes only through the  %% words: - (an enumeration of limits)
target, so the test cannot separate extrapolation distance from properties of
the target. References at small targets are weak: at \confDetMinTarget\ only
\confDetMin\% of target pairs are determined, against \confDetTop\% at
\targetScaleLabel, which expected-error scoring charges for rather than ignores.
Second, the response is not linear. The following summaries were computed after
the review, without intervals or pre-registration: within each top rung with
at least three targets the slope on lever arm ranges from \phPerTopMin\ to  %% words: - (a minimum number of targets)
\phPerTopMax\ points per decade, smaller at larger top rungs; restricted to the
\phStrongN\ designs with targets of \phStrongFrom\ or more, the joint lever-arm
coefficient is \phStrongLever; on the \doseDesigns\ nested designs, without
adjustment, the slope is \phNestedSlope. Third, rung count here is the lower end
of an unevenly spaced window, so it also changes the fitted span. In
semi-synthetic worlds with C4's noise and recipe-specific misspecification, each
factor varied alone around one base design gives slopes of \synthLeverSlope\ per
decade of lever arm, \synthTopSlope\ per decade of top rung and
\synthRungsSlope\ per rung (Monte Carlo precision, not uncertainty about real
data); the top-rung and rung-count responses are not monotone, and without
noise the rung-count slope changes sign (\cref{app:confound}).

\paragraph{Mechanism.} Semi-synthetic fixed-candidate worlds that differ only
in the truth separate candidate sources (\cref{tab:arms}). They are simulations
built from the DataDecide curves, not interventions on training. With pure
power-law truths and no noise, projection's form is correct by construction, so
it captures every crossover and its excess falls as the lever arm grows
(Spearman \noiselessRhoArmA); this arm checks the simulator. Adding seed noise
alone reverses the sign (\rhoArmA). Recipe-specific misspecification alone,
without noise, also reverses it (\noiselessRhoArmBTwo). Misspecification shared
by all recipes changes the noisy result little (\rhoArmBOne\ against \rhoArmA),
although without noise it moves the correlation from \noiselessRhoArmA\ to
\noiselessRhoArmBOne: in a pairwise order it largely cancels, and on real C4
its amplitude-sharing cancellation ratio is \cancelCFourRatio, partial rather
than complete. The
world with all misfit and noise reproduces \attrRealTotal\% of the real slope.
A sequential decomposition of that simulated slope, which depends on the order
of the arms and is not identified, gives with a power law as the reference form
\attrPowerVar\% to the variance arm, \attrPowerSpecific\% to recipe-specific
misspecification, \attrPowerCommon\% to common-mode misspecification and
\attrPowerInter\% to their interaction (the run of \cref{tab:arms} gives
\attrArmsCommon\% and \attrArmsInter\% for the last two); the variance arm alone
is \attrRealVar\% of the real slope. With a Chinchilla-form reference the shares
are \attrChinVar\%, \attrChinSpecific\%, \attrChinCommon\% and \attrChinInter\%.
These are shares of the lever-arm slope in decision error, not of fit error, so
they do not contradict the dominance of misspecification in the fit below. The
pre-registered prediction that the Spearman correlation rises monotonically
with the misfit scale \armMonotoneLambda.

\paragraph{Error structure.} Misspecification dominates the fit error: residual
scatter is \residRatio\ times the seed standard error, a variance inflation of
\varInflation. It is structured in compute. On DataDecide, tokens per parameter
drift with model size and correlate with compute at \tokensPerParamCorr;
adding that drift as a regressor reduces the absolute extrapolation error at
the target by \tokensPerParamReduction\%. Whether misspecification cancels between two
recipes is decided by whether its amplitude is shared. In a synthetic control,
a shared amplitude leaves the cancellation residual at or below
\cancelSharedMax\ for coefficients of variation of the exponent up to
\cancelAlphaMax, and a
per-recipe amplitude destroys cancellation (at least \cancelPerMin) at every
spread. Differences in curvature alone do not decide it, and no normalisation
of the metric removes it.

\paragraph{Where the effect is inconclusive.} On OLMES macro error the
association is \lamOlmesVerdict\ (Spearman \lamOlmesRho, $p=\lamOlmesP$ against
the calibrated null), and on correct-answer probability it is
\lamCpVerdict\ (\lamCpRho, $p=\lamCpP$); both point estimates have the
opposite sign to C4's, so the association is metric-dependent. A margin-based
metric shows it over all designs (\lamMarginRho, $p\le\lamMarginP$, the Monte
Carlo floor), outside the corrected family, but reverses on the
\lamMarginSubN\ designs whose rungs are all above chance (\lamMarginSubRho,
$p=\lamMarginSubP$). Across \specN\ analysis specifications
(\cref{fig:spec-curve}), scored by hard mis-selection against the observed
target order rather than by expected error, projection is worse in \specPositive,
ties in \specTies\ and is nominally better in \specBelow, all of them on OLMES
metrics; the specifications share data and many give identical results, so these
counts are not independent evidence. Reassigning
C4's recipe-specific residuals among recipes never makes projection win at any
of \mechSweepPoints\ points, while OLMES residuals placed into C4 can. What
distinguishes the metrics is the shape of their recipe-specific residuals,
which none of \statsTriedAll\ summary statistics tried across the pre-registered
rounds captures. One, the size of the top-rung residual, tracks the excess (Spearman
\statsNearMissRho) but was chosen after seeing the data and has
counterexamples.

\section{Consequences for selection and uncertainty}
\label{sec:consequences}

\paragraph{Single-scale ranking is the baseline to beat.} \Cref{tab:advantage}
gives each estimator's and allocation's difference from single-scale ranking with
its calibrated random-candidate interval. On C4 the intervals exclude an advantage
over single-scale ranking larger than \advExcludeCFourMax\ points for every
estimator and allocation, and on OLMES error larger than \advExcludeOlmesMax\
points; smaller advantages are not excluded. The tests are weak: against each
estimator's effect in the benchmark, calibrated power is \advPowerMin\ to
\advPowerMax, below one half for every estimator; none was computed for the
allocations. On the design that
fits up to \flexFitTop\ and targets \flexTarget, single-scale ranking mis-selects
\flexSingle\% of pairs; the shared-exponent fit \flexShared\%, plain projection
\flexPlain\% and a multi-family ensemble \flexEnsemble\% (\cref{fig:flexibility}).
Across the restated claims, every nominally significant comparison of an
estimator or allocation with single-scale ranking has single-scale ranking
ahead; we attach no test to that direction pattern, because the comparisons
share recipes. The ensemble's deficit is the one estimator comparison that
survives correction in the fixed-candidate family ($p=\ensFixedP$). Allocating
runs across budgets does not help either: under a least-squares projection, the
design that minimises the variance of the target level minimises every pair's
mis-selection probability at once if the model is well specified
(\cref{app:design}), which on C4 it is not (\cref{sec:lever}), and the optimised
allocation is nominally worse than single-scale ranking at the smallest budget
fraction, and its intervals at larger fractions are in \cref{tab:advantage}. Common random
numbers would reduce gap variance if seed deviations were shared across
recipes. A pre-registered pilot on a laptop-scale model, comparing learning-rate
multipliers rather than data recipes, found no large reduction: the median
gap-variance ratio was \crnMedianRatio\ ([\crnLow, \crnHigh]) against a
decision rule of at most \crnRule. The interval does not exclude a moderate
reduction; the programme was closed.

\paragraph{False certification by metric and lever arm.} A certification rule
certifies a pair's order only if it holds at level $1-\alpha$ under every form in  %% nolint: algebra
a form set, with variance from propagated seed error, optionally plus a
recipe-specific misfit term $\kappa L$ whose $\kappa$ is estimated on held-out
rungs below the target with leave-one-ladder-out cross-validation
(\cref{alg:certify}). We ran it over \stageDesigns\ DataDecide designs at
$\alpha=\stageAlpha\%$ and describe its realized false certification by metric
and lever-arm group (\cref{tab:cert-metric}); the split was not pre-registered
and carries no test. On C4, certification from seed error alone stays at or
below \certLevCFourSeedMax\% in every group (\certLevCFourSeedShort\%,
\certLevCFourSeedMiddle\% and \certLevCFourSeedLong\% from short to long lever
arms), and with the misfit term at \certLevCFourSelShort\%,
\certLevCFourSelMiddle\% and \certLevCFourSelLong\%. On OLMES error, seed-error
certification is at \certLevErrSeedShort\%, \certLevErrSeedMiddle\% and
\certLevErrSeedLong\%, and with the misfit term \certLevErrSelShort\%,
\certLevErrSelMiddle\% and \certLevErrSelLong\%, above nominal from the middle
group on. On correct-answer probability, seed error gives
\certLevCpSeedShort\%, \certLevCpSeedMiddle\% and \certLevCpSeedLong\%, and the
misfit term \certLevCpSelShort\%, \certLevCpSelMiddle\% and \certLevCpSelLong\%.
Pooled over metrics (\cref{fig:certify}), seed-error certification rises from
\stageLevShortSeed\% to \stageLevLongSeed\%, as a pre-registered prediction
stated; the rise comes from the OLMES metrics, not from C4. Each metric's groups
hold \certLevCFourNShort, \certLevCFourNMiddle\ and \certLevCFourNLong\ designs.

\paragraph{The pre-registered selector criterion failed.} We also
pre-registered a criterion for the rule as a selector: realized false
certification at or below nominal, and higher coverage than every baseline that
also meets nominal. The first part held (\stageSelFcr\% at \stageSelCov\%
coverage); the second failed. Certification from seed error alone met nominal
when pooled over metrics (\stageSeedFcr\%) with higher coverage
(\stageSeedCov\%). It exceeds nominal on both OLMES metrics (\stageErrSeedFcr\%
and \stageCpSeedFcr\%), but the pooled criterion was the pre-registered one. The
misfit rule itself exceeds nominal on OLMES macro error (\stageErrSelFcr\%),
where the candidate-level upper bound on its excess over nominal is
\stageErrSelExcessUp\ points per pair; pooled, that bound is
\stageSelExcessUp. On C4, single-scale ranking meets nominal
at full coverage (\stageCFourSingleFcr\%), so no certifying rule can improve on
it there. We do not propose the rule as a method, and we did not iterate on
it after the criterion failed (\cref{tab:stage}). The pre-registration and its deviations
are in \cref{app:stage}.

\section{Case study: the Marin speedrun leaderboard}
\label{sec:marin}

The Marin speedrun \citep{marin2025speedrun} ranks \marinSubmissions\
submissions, each trained at four sizes, by a fitted power law's forecast at  %% words: - (the number of rungs on the leaderboard ladder)
\marinForecastX\ FLOPs, a lever arm of more than one decade beyond the largest
run. Each size is a single run. These results are specific to this leaderboard.

\paragraph{A convergence defect and its fix.} The leaderboard's
Levenberg--Marquardt fit runs on raw FLOPs and stops on an absolute error
tolerance. Its forecasts depart from the least-squares optimum by up to
\marinDepartMax\ BPB (median \marinDepartMedian). On an earlier snapshot of the
leaderboard, rescaling FLOPs alone brings \marinScaledOnlyShare\% of
\marinScaledOnlyFits\ multi-start fits to the optimum; rescaling together with a
relative tolerance brings \marinMultistartReached\ of \marinMultistartFits\
multi-start fits on the official set there, within
\marinMultistartDepart\ BPB. The fix changes
\marinPairsChanged\ of \marinPairs\ pairwise orders (Kendall $\tau$
\marinKendall) and the submission in place \marinChangedPlace.

\paragraph{What the forecasts resolve.} Single-run noise at these sizes is not
measured. Borrowed seed standard deviations put it at
\noiseLowMin--\noiseLowMax\% relative, and leave-one-out bounds from public
sweeps put it below \marinBoundLow--\marinBoundHigh\% by size
(\cref{app:marin}). Across two converged forms, \marinFormCore\ of \marinPairs\
pairs keep their order and only the top submission, \marinFormTopOne, is
invariant. Propagating noise at the upper bound, \marinHold\ pairs hold in at
least \marinHoldLevel\% of draws, the leading submission is first in
\marinTopOneShare\% of draws, and no top-$k$ set holds counting from the top
(\cref{fig:marin}). Requiring both form agreement and noise robustness at
\marinSigmaStar\% relative noise, the level at which certification against a
held-out rung is calibrated and which exceeds the single-run bracket,
certifies \marinCertified\ pairs. Of these, \marinCertifiedVsLast\ place a
submission above the last-placed one, and none places one submission above all
others.

\paragraph{Protocol changes between rungs.} Every submission changes its batch
size somewhere on the ladder: \marinBatchAtThird\ double it at the third rung
and \marinBatchAtTop\ at the top rung. For
\marinStepMarked\ of \marinSubmissions\ submissions, allowing a step at the
batch change moves the forecast by more than one gap between neighbouring
submissions. The misfit-term rule, calibrated on the rung below the target,
certifies \stageMarinCert\ pairs here with \stageMarinFcr\% false
certification: misfit calibrated below the target cannot anticipate a protocol
change at the target. Against single-scale ranking's \marinProjSingle\ point errors in
\marinPairs\ pairs at a held-out rung, the leaderboard's own form makes
\marinOwnFormWrong\ ($|t|=\marinOwnFormT$ against a critical value of
\marinProjCrit\ calibrated on DataDecide and transferred, so indicative) and
the shared exponent \marinProjShared: a difference of \marinSharedDiff\ points,
interval [\marinSharedLow, \marinSharedHigh], which excludes only advantages
larger than \marinSharedExclude\ points. No power calculation exists for this
design.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

The evidence is one public suite with seed replicates and one intervention
class, data recipes, plus one leaderboard without replicates. The lever-arm effect
is established on C4 bits per token; on OLMES accuracy metrics it is
inconclusive, and its attribution between variance and misspecification depends
on the reference functional form. The confound test adjusts lever arm for
top-rung size and rung count, but not for the target's size or DataDecide's
drift in tokens per parameter, which moves with compute, and its linear model
does not fit a response that saturates; its calibrated critical values come from a
benchmark whose true effects are smaller than the real estimates, and
references at small targets are weakly determined. Of \ledgerHeld\ plus
\ledgerFailed\ predictions recorded across the project, \ledgerFailed\ failed;
\cref{app:ledger} lists them. The calibration benchmark's truths are
shrunk seed-mean curves with Gaussian noise; the fixed-candidate procedure
covers \calFlatFixedCov\ when the noise profile is flatter than DataDecide's,
against \calControlFixedCov\ in the control (\cref{app:regime}).
Critical values come from a few hundred worlds per family. On Marin, single-run  %% words: ~\calibRandomWorlds
noise is bracketed, not measured, and protocol changes between rungs confound
every rung comparison. We ran no new training beyond a laptop-scale pilot.

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Scaling-law fitting and forecasting.} Power-law fits to loss
\citep{kaplan2020scaling,hoffmann2022training} are sensitive to fitting choices
\citep{besiroglu2024chinchilla,choshen2024hitchhiker}, and extrapolation to
downstream tasks needs care \citep{gadre2024language,bhagia2024establishing}.
Small-scale forecasts of larger models \citep{ivgi2022scaling,ruan2024observational}
and hyperparameter transfer across width \citep{yang2022tensor}, which bears on
per-size protocol changes, are related. We study the decisions such fits
support rather than their fit error.

\paragraph{Small-to-large decisions.} DataDecide \citep{magnusson2025datadecide}
and Signal and Noise \citep{heineman2025signal} measure how well small runs
predict large-scale orderings, and optimizer benchmarks compare rules across
sizes \citep{wen2025fantastic}. They report point estimates; we add
candidate-level inference and the lever-arm structure.

\paragraph{Selective prediction and multiple comparisons.} Abstaining when a
decision is not resolved follows selective classification
\citep{geifman2017selective} and conformal prediction
\citep{vovk2005algorithmic,angelopoulos2021gentle}. Variance in benchmarks
\citep{bouthillier2021accounting,madaan2024quantifying} and label error in
references \citep{northcutt2021pervasive} motivate treating the reference as
uncertain. Low statistical power in evaluation comparisons
\citep{card2020little,dror2018hitchhiker} and seed variance in fine-tuning
\citep{dodge2020fine} are documented for NLP. Selecting the best of several
populations and inference on ranks have a long statistical literature
\citep{gupta1965some,hsu1996multiple,mogstad2024inference}; the possibly-best
set and the Marin rank intervals are instances. Family-wide correction uses
Holm and Benjamini--Yekutieli.
