# The structure of scaling-law model error

Generated by `scripts/render_consolidated_findings.py` from committed JSON.

Three results about the *structure* of the error a scaling-law fit makes, rather than its size. The unifying claim: **misspecification structure, not geometry, determines which corrections can work, and the structure is measurable in advance.** Each result below pairs a measurement with a practical consequence.

The starting point is that misspecification dominates: residual scatter of cell means about a fitted power law is **2.90x** the seed standard error, an **8.4x** variance inflation. The dominant term in projection variance is therefore functional-form error, which more seeds do not shrink - not parameter noise, which they would.

## 1. The error is compute-structured, so it cannot be absorbed into a noise term

The obvious repair for a fit that misses its nominal noise is to inflate the noise: model the residual as `sigma_seed^2 + phi * r^2` and estimate one scalar `phi`. That fails, and the failure is informative.

| test | result |
|---|---|
| one `phi` transferred across ladders | over-states variance by 2.7x to 8.9x |
| does `phi` track design geometry? | monotone in log L (slope -0.045) and in k (slope +0.054) |
| does it predict held-out structure? | under-predicts residual variance at an unseen scale by 12x |

If residuals were exchangeable noise, one inflator would serve every ladder, would not track ladder geometry, and would extrapolate. None holds.

**Consequence.** Variance inflation is the first thing a practitioner reaches for when a fit does not match its nominal noise. This says that reflex fails here, and says why, which is more useful than the extension would have been had it worked. The correct object is a model of the compute-dependent structure, not a scalar.

## 2. The error's amplitude is shared on some metrics and per-intervention on others, which determines whether differencing can cancel it

Writing each intervention's curve as `y_i(C) = f_i(C) + m(C) + e_i(C)`, a shared misspecification `m(C)` cancels in the gap `y_i - y_j`. Whether it does is measurable, via the cancellation ratio `Var(r_i - r_j) / (Var(r_i) + Var(r_j))` - about 1 under independent residuals, about 0 under perfect common mode.

| suite | fit to | cancellation | PC1 loading CV | structure |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `datadecide_c4_bits_per_token` | 300M | **0.147** | 0.156 | offset |
| `datadecide_c4_bits_per_token` | 150M | **0.234** | 0.219 | offset |
| `datadecide_olmes_macro_error` | 300M | **0.130** | 0.145 | offset |
| `datadecide_olmes_macro_error` | 150M | **0.192** | 0.182 | offset |
| `datadecide_olmes_correct_prob` | 300M | 0.707 | 2.032 | scaled |
| `datadecide_olmes_correct_prob` | 150M | 0.772 | 1.398 | scaled |
| `signal_and_noise_c4_bits_per_byte` | 300M | 0.817 | 1.370 | scaled |
| `signal_and_noise_c4_bits_per_byte` | 150M | 0.916 | 2.153 | scaled |

**The diagnostic.** The PC1 loading coefficient of variation separates the two groups with no overlap: **0.145-0.219** for the cancelling suites versus **1.370-2.153** for the non-cancelling ones. Every suite has a dominant first principal component (0.80-0.97 of residual variance), so a large PC1 is *not* sufficient evidence - the loading spread is the test. `r_i = m + e_i` cancels; `r_i = a_i m + e_i` leaves `(a_i - a_j) m`.

**The mechanism, established against two rival explanations.** A normalisation artifact was ruled out: no space rescues the scaled signature (cancellation stays at 0.831 (absolute), 0.817 (relative), 0.924 (log)). Curvature spread was ruled out as a *cause* by a control in which the geometry is known: holding the misspecification amplitude shared, cancellation stays at 0.000-0.002 across a true exponent spread of 0 to 0.6, while making the amplitude per-intervention destroys cancellation (1.01-1.03) at every exponent spread.

What governs it is **whether the misspecification amplitude is shared across interventions.** The correlation between loadings and fitted exponents on the real suites (r = -0.81 and -0.85) is a symptom rather than a cause: a per-intervention amplitude biases each intervention's fitted exponent, so loadings and exponents co-vary without either causing the other.

**Consequence.** A differenced estimator, which models the gap rather than the levels, is correctly specified only where the amplitude is shared. That is checkable before building anything, from the loading CV. It is also why we did not build one: its advantage is largest where single-scale ranking is already near-optimal, and it fails where amplitudes differ - which is where fitted trends diverge, where crossovers live, and where beating the baseline would matter.

## 3. When the error is shared and interventions differ in level, a badly biased fit still orders correctly

A defective fit configuration pinned the decay exponent at a bound three orders of magnitude from its true value on an accuracy metric, for every intervention. The corrected re-run reproduced **every** decision number.

| metric | old alpha | new alpha | median projection change | as % of between-intervention spread | Spearman(old, new) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `c4_en_bits_per_token` | [0.1385, 0.1649] | [0.1385, 0.1649] | 1.27e-07 | 0.0% | 1.000000 |
| `olmes_macro_correct_prob_per_char_deficit` | [0.0713, 0.4585] | [0.0713, 0.4585] | 4.00e-08 | 0.0% | 1.000000 |
| `olmes_macro_error` | [0.0500, 0.0500] | [0.0171, 0.0337] | 6.49e-03 | 23.8% | 1.000000 |

On the accuracy metric the projections moved by a quarter of the entire between-intervention spread and the ranking was *identical*, because the bias was common to all interventions and cancelled in comparisons. That is a property of the geometry, not a general licence - demonstrated, not asserted:

| geometry | pairs reordered per trial | trials with identical ordering |
|---|---|---|
| interventions differ in **level** | 0.017 | 98% |
| interventions differ in **curvature** | 1.150 | 33% |

The same defect reorders **69x more pairs** under curvature geometry, with both arms matched on target spread and on having true exponents below the defective bound.

**Consequence, two-sided.** An estimator can be badly wrong in parameter space and exactly right in decision space, which argues for evaluating decisions rather than fit quality. The same fact bounds what a good decision number certifies: decision accuracy is *insensitive* to real estimator defects, so it validates the decision on this suite under this geometry - not the estimator, and not transportability. Decision-level evaluation needs parameter-level diagnostics alongside it.

## Summary

| result | measurement | practical consequence |
|---|---|---|
| error is compute-structured | one `phi` off by 2.7-8.9x across ladders, 12x on held-out | variance inflation cannot repair a misspecified fit |
| amplitude sharing is metric-dependent | loading CV 0.145-0.219 vs 1.370-2.153, no overlap | differencing works only where the CV is low, checkable in advance |
| shared error + level geometry cancels | projections move 24% of spread, Spearman 1.000000 | fit quality does not certify decision quality, in either direction |
